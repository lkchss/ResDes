# Critique and refinement

Use the [academic design context](design-evidence/design-context.md) to judge the intended research task. Separate scientific integrity, destination compliance and academic communication quality. Prefer a concrete comparison problem to a generic style preference. Check that every allegation agrees with visible titles, notes and evidence; label conditional advice as conditional. A shorter or less decorated display is not automatically better.

Inspect the actual artifact in context, preferably rendered at intended size. Identify its intended claim, audience and publication context; distinguish a visibly observed defect from an inference or missing information. If only a screenshot or draft is available, limit the review accordingly.

Prioritize findings by consequence:

1. **Integrity blockers:** misleading or unverifiable evidence, including ambiguous units, denominators, uncertainty, transformations, category mapping or exclusions. State what evidence is needed to resolve each issue.
2. **Applicable requirements and defaults:** specific destination or editor requirements and relevant conventions, with source and stage where known. Distinguish requirements from recommendations and flag unverified rules or conflicts.
3. **Academic communication:** identify where the intended comparison requires avoidable search, decoding or reconstruction. Recommend a specific arrangement, encoding or hierarchy change and explain its tradeoff. Treat this as part of artifact quality, while distinguishing consequential obstacles from optional taste. Correct numbers and polished typography do not establish effective communication.

## Read the artifact before accepting it

Use the actual render and its caption/notes, not the implementation's intentions:

- Decode one representative cell or mark completely: quantity, statistic, unit, group and denominator. Where did you have to look? Put shared interpretation at the closest useful scope when this removes recurring lookup; keep necessary qualifications available.
- Trace the intended comparison, pointing to the actual entries or marks. Identify avoidable search or reconstruction. Retain effective adjacency and scales; do not reward novelty alone.
- Separate what changes from what stays constant. Repeated metadata can obscure the changing evidence. Move a genuinely shared count, unit or definition to its header/group note when it remains unambiguous; check exceptions before consolidating. Necessary repeated identifiers and page-break headers are not clutter.
- Match every caption/legend claim to visible marks: open/filled, line style, color, summary versus observation, and uncertainty. Inspect the appearance rather than trusting a declaration in code. Check limits, glyphs and cropping too.

For each consequential finding, retain a short location → reading problem → proposed change in the existing working notes or final explanation. When creating or refining, implement the justified correction, rerender, and check that location again. Verify preserved values and qualifications. A finding is not resolved by merely mentioning it in the handoff. Keep this to one correction pass; report any remaining issue honestly. If the artifact already serves the reader, say so with concrete evidence rather than forcing a cosmetic change.
