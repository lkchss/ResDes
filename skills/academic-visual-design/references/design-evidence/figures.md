# Figure design: decision context

These are conditional design judgments, not universal journal rules. Evidence types and limits appear in [figure-sources.json](figure-sources.json). The strongest figure makes the intended comparison easy while exposing what was measured, estimated, and omitted.

## Choose the visual operation

- **Compare values:** use aligned positions on a shared scale when precise pairwise comparison matters. Good: a dot-and-interval plot of treatment effects with a common reference line. Weak: detached circles of varying area when the reader must compare close values. Cleveland and McGill tested elementary numerical judgments, not all analytic or aesthetic tasks [F1](https://faculty.washington.edu/aragon/classes/hcde511/s12/readings/cleveland84.pdf).
- **Show change or ordering:** a time series or ordered line makes continuity and turning points visible when the x variable is ordered and connecting observations is meaningful. For unordered groups, avoid connecting marks unless the connection explicitly encodes a real relation such as matching or pairing; otherwise the line implies an unsupported path. If a complex pattern is the question, retain the detail needed to see it rather than minimizing indiscriminately. [F1](https://faculty.washington.edu/aragon/classes/hcde511/s12/readings/cleveland84.pdf); synthesis.
- **Show distributions:** for small continuous samples, plot individual observations and a meaningful summary. A mean and error bar alone can conceal outliers, clusters, or skew [F2](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article/file?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pbio.1002128&type=printable). With many observations, use density, bins, or quantiles and disclose their settings; a cloud of overlapping points may hide density. A density shape can itself mislead with tiny samples or an arbitrary bandwidth.
- **Show within-unit change:** connect each subject’s before/after observations when the pairing is meaningful and readable; otherwise show the distribution of within-subject differences or carefully sampled trajectories. Separate marginal group summaries can hide whether individuals moved together or in opposite directions. Do not connect different subjects merely because their points occupy adjacent categories [F2](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article/file?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pbio.1002128&type=printable); synthesis.

## Make the inference legible

- **Name uncertainty explicitly:** identify the mark, interval type and level, estimand, units, sample or independent-unit count, and any adjustment in the figure or caption (synthesis). SD describes observed spread, while SE describes uncertainty in a stated estimator (for example, an estimated mean); confidence and prediction intervals answer different questions. Cumming and Finch explain that interpreting separate confidence intervals depends on design and interval properties: overlap does not directly test a difference, and paired data require the covariance of observations. When the contrast matters, show its estimate and interval [F9](https://courses.media.mit.edu/2010spring/mas964/Week3/cumming2005.pdf). In psychology, the 1999 APA task force recommended plotting interval estimates where possible [F3](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/amp-54-8-594.pdf); this is historical expert guidance, not an experiment or current production specification.
- **Choose uncertainty encoding for the audience and question:** in crowdsourced inference experiments, bar-plus-error encodings induced biases, while continuous gradient and violin-like encodings reduced certain biases; the latter represented uncertainty in a mean, not necessarily the empirical distribution of observations [F4](https://vis.csail.mit.edu/classes/6.859/readings/pdfs/Corell-ErrorBarsConsideredHarmful.pdf). Good: a labelled point and CI for expert coefficient comparisons, optionally augmented by a distribution when appropriate. Weak: unlabeled whiskers treated as self-explanatory. Do not imply an unfamiliar gradient will automatically improve every task.
- **Clinical/JAMA context:** for a binary clinical outcome, report group event counts or absolute rates and the effect with interval, adding relative effects where appropriate; an impressive relative reduction without baseline risk can obscure clinical magnitude. The CONSORT 2025 checklist is a reporting guideline for randomized trials, not a universal figure template [F5](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2832868).
- **Economics/AER context:** an event-study display can show estimates and intervals before and after an intervention, a zero reference, event time, and the omitted reference period. Do not say that insignificant pre-event coefficients prove parallel trends: low power and selection on a pretest can mislead [F6](https://www.aeaweb.org/content/file?id=15847). Methods, estimand, comparison group and interval construction still need explanation; visual form cannot validate a causal design.

## Scales, facets, color, and labels

- Use a zero baseline when bar length encodes magnitude; for point or line plots, a nonzero range can expose meaningful small changes, but disclose limits and avoid implying the visible span is the whole magnitude. Use a log scale for multiplicative comparisons over orders of magnitude and label the transformation and units. A log scale cannot directly display zero or negative values. These are synthesis decisions, not conclusions established by a single perception experiment.
- Use shared facet scales for direct magnitude comparisons of commensurable quantities with compatible units. Independent scales can reveal within-panel structure when ranges differ substantially, but mark that choice clearly and do not invite direct comparison of apparent heights or slopes. Equal visual slopes across different scales need not represent equal rates.
- For ordered scalar fields use a perceptually ordered sequential map; for deviation around a meaningful reference use a diverging map centered there; for cyclic variables use a cyclic map. Rainbow-like uneven maps can create apparent boundaries; check color-vision accessibility and grayscale and use redundant labels, line styles or shapes for categories [F7](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-19160-7.pdf). A diverging palette centered on an arbitrary midpoint gives that midpoint unwarranted meaning.
- Make the reading order match the analytical order: panel identifiers, condition names, axes and units must remain distinguishable at final size; connect series directly where possible, or place the legend close enough to decode reliably. Label a reference or intervention at its location rather than forcing repeated searching. Keep context and meaningful gridlines when they help estimation; omit visual elements only when they add no useful information. These are synthesis recommendations; destination-specific production requirements must be checked separately.

## Images and diagrams are evidence too

- **Life-science images:** show scale bars, relevant channels and comparable acquisition/display conditions; disclose adjustments and crops. Preserve originals. Whole-image tonal adjustments can aid visibility but must not erase or fabricate features; do not splice fields into a single apparent field or selectively alter a feature [F8](https://rupress.org/jcb/article/166/1/11/34064/What-s-in-a-picture-The-temptation-of-image). Image-specific journal policies govern particular submissions. Good: visibly delineated montage with stated processing. Weak: an unmarked composite presented as one micrograph.
- **Nonquantitative diagrams:** Larkin and Simon model how spatial grouping can make relevant information available together and reduce search in illustrative mathematics and physics problems; the benefit depends on the representation and task [F10](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1551-6708.1987.tb00863.x). As a design synthesis, use boxes, arrows and grouping to encode actual entities, relationships, direction, and sequence. Make branch conditions and feedback loops explicit; avoid decorative arrows that imply causation or temporal order. In randomized trials, the CONSORT 2025 example distinguishes enrollment, allocation, follow-up and analysis, with counts and losses; adapt to the real design rather than forcing it into two arms [F5](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2832868). The diagram supports auditability, not proof that the trial was unbiased.
- **IEEE/engineering and ACM/computing contexts:** a system or algorithm diagram should distinguish data flow, control flow and system boundaries; a performance graph should specify metric, workload, baseline, units, aggregation and uncertainty where relevant. A normalized speedup plot needs its baseline and direction stated; a schematic should not suggest a measured dependency that was never tested. These are cross-domain synthesis examples, not claims of distinct journal aesthetics or official requirements.

**Transfer limits:** studies examine particular readers, datasets and tasks. A chart effective for close numerical extraction may not be best for seeing a distribution, describing a mechanism, or conveying uncertainty. Test representative judgments with intended readers when the decision is consequential; no design rescues a flawed analysis.
