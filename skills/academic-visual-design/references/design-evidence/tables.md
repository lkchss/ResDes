# Academic tables: decisions before decoration

This is a bounded research draft. Bracketed IDs link to primary sources; inspection states and limits are in [table-sources.json](table-sources.json). Conditional rules and examples are **synthesis** unless an empirical result or a scoped venue instruction is explicitly identified; a citation does not mean every example was experimentally tested.

## Choose the reader’s job

For lookup, make identifiers and exact values easy to locate; for comparison, place the values to be compared adjacent in a consistent direction. In simulation studies, method comparisons may drive the arrangement of methods, while estimands, scenarios, performance measures and Monte Carlo uncertainty compete for space [T3](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.8086). If the central claim is a trajectory or distribution, consider a figure alongside a small table for exact values; if the job is checking many distinct values, retain the table. This is a task choice, not a promise that figures always outperform tables.

**Evidence boundary:** Dolan and colleagues compared formats for judgments about screening outcomes: across their tested outcomes the table, flow chart and icon array had better comparative accuracy than the tested bar chart and risk scale, but one outcome reversed the table’s standing; ordinal accuracy did not differ significantly [T2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22618998/). These are particular risk displays and tasks, not a ranking of all graphs and tables. Remshard and Queenborough surveyed 1,068 ecology/evolution tables and assembled design guidance; the survey describes publication practice, not a randomized test of reader performance [T1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1002/ece3.10062).

## Structure the path through the values

- **Orient to comparison.** If readers compare treatments within each outcome, put treatment values side by side; if they compare outcomes within each treatment, transpose or rearrange. Bad: corresponding estimates separated by unrelated columns. Better: group each estimate with the comparator and its uncertainty, retaining the same order across outcomes. The ecology guide favors vertical comparisons [T1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1002/ece3.10062), whereas the JAMA chapter emphasizes horizontal primary comparisons [T4](https://academic.oup.com/book/58841/chapter/489872324). These are task-dependent expert recommendations, not a universal experimental ranking; choose for the specific comparison and available space.
- **Use hierarchy to clarify, not decorate.** A descriptive stub identifies each row; spanners express real shared groupings, while subheads define each column. Indentation or spacing can distinguish a parent category from its levels. Repeat necessary identifiers where splitting or pagination would otherwise sever the relationship. Avoid a spanner for columns that have no common meaning, or repeating a unit in every cell when it can be stated unambiguously in a heading. Native header associations must still be checked; a visually complex spanner may be harder to navigate than separate simpler tables.
- **Align and round within a meaningful measure group.** Align comparable numeric values by decimal place or consistently right-align using tabular numerals; align labels for scanning. Use precision justified by measurement and interpretation, consistently for comparable values, rather than forcing counts, percentages and estimates to share one decimal rule. Bad: `12.4, 12.38571, 12.40` for equivalent measurements; better: an explicitly justified common display precision. Do not round away a meaningful difference or report an estimate as zero because of display rounding [T1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1002/ece3.10062), [T5](https://academic.oup.com/pan/pages/Instructions_To_Authors).
- **Notes are part of the data model.** Define units, sample denominators, missing-value symbols, abbreviations, the meaning of parentheses, and uncertainty or emphasis where relevant. Put shared information in headings and exceptions in marked notes; do not bury an assumption that changes the interpretation in a symbol-only key. A blank may mean unavailable, not applicable, or zero; label it. The legacy, undated OUP Political Analysis page advocated self-explanatory displays, units and uncertainty; it is historical design advice here, not verified current submission authority [T5](https://academic.oup.com/pan/pages/Instructions_To_Authors).
- **Manage density before shrinking.** Remove duplicate prose and nonessential fields, group related rows, or split by a meaningful research question; keep comparisons that require adjacency together. Move detailed archival data or robustness results to an accessible supplement, with a pointer from the main table. A large matrix can be justified when cross-cell lookup is the task; splitting it without repeated labels or a useful index can increase cognitive work. The chapter in the JAMA Editors Guide distinguishes archival and presentation tables and emphasizes matching structure to purpose [T4](https://academic.oup.com/book/58841/chapter/489872324); balance accessibility and comparison adjacency instead of making a blanket rule to split.

## Domain tasks and concrete patterns

**Psychology and social science.** APA describes numerical and textual tables, including means and standard deviations and stimulus lists [T9](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/tables-figures). For a study-characteristics table, let readers identify the sample, group, denominator and distribution; pair means with SD or medians with a suitable spread, explicitly named. Bad: a column titled “value” mixing n, percentages and means. Better: a labelled statistic or well-defined grouped columns. This is a general design example, not an APA-mandated template [T4](https://academic.oup.com/book/58841/chapter/489872324), [T9](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/tables-figures).

**Clinical and biomedical.** A baseline characteristics table helps assess who was studied; outcome tables instead foreground treatment contrasts, estimates and intervals. Identify analysis population, group denominators, units and whether an interval describes variability or estimate uncertainty. A baseline SD describes participant spread; a confidence interval around an effect describes estimation uncertainty, so they are not interchangeable. The chapter in the JAMA Editors Guide highlights self-contained cells and the distinction between descriptive and results tables [T4](https://academic.oup.com/book/58841/chapter/489872324).

**Economics and regression.** For an AER-style empirical regression task, arrange model specifications in a purposeful sequence, with the same coefficient on the same row, and disclose dependent variable, unit, sample size, controls, fixed effects, uncertainty type and clustering as relevant. This arrangement is cross-disciplinary synthesis. The AER style guide specifically requires standard errors in parentheses and prohibits significance asterisks for estimation results [T8](https://www.aeaweb.org/journals/aer/style-guide). A coefficient and its standard error are not substitutes for explaining the estimand or sample; asterisks alone do not express magnitude or uncertainty. The legacy OUP Political Analysis page similarly advocated reporting uncertainty rather than routine significance stars [T5](https://academic.oup.com/pan/pages/Instructions_To_Authors). A coefficient plot may communicate the main contrast more quickly; retain a numerical table when exact coefficients or specifications matter.

**Computing and engineering.** For ablations, compare the full system and clearly defined removals on the same datasets and metrics. Indicate metric direction, evaluation protocol and the meaning of variability when available; make baseline and changed component legible. Chen et al.’s ACL example places the complete system and component removals in rows and nests prediction tasks under datasets; this shows one actual architecture, not proof that it is optimal [T6](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.685.pdf). For engineering experiments, group measurement conditions and units so readers can compare performance against a reference without confusing test regimes; report relevant uncertainty. IEEE Transactions on Professional Communication has its own table title, note and line conventions, including optional internal vertical lines for wider tables [T7](https://procomm.ieee.org/transactions-of-professional-communication/for-prospective-authors/guidelines-to-follow/guidelines-for-formatting-manuscripts/). Those rules apply to that venue, not all IEEE publications or engineering research.

**Life science.** A sample or assay table may have different units across rows, whereas an ablation or assay-comparison matrix may benefit from dataset or condition spanners; the organizing principle is the comparison rather than a journal’s visual identity [T1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1002/ece3.10062), [T6](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.685.pdf). Nature Communications’ formatting instructions call for editable Word or LaTeX tables and restrict some styling; this is a journal-specific production requirement [T10](https://www.nature.com/documents/ncomms-formatting-instructions.pdf).

## Source and handoff

Keep underlying values, definitions, code or formulas and an editable, structured table as the source of truth; generate publication outputs from it and check the final proof against the source. Semantic table headers, navigable reading order and notes matter in addition to visual appearance. Nature Communications specifies editable table submission [T10](https://www.nature.com/documents/ncomms-formatting-instructions.pdf). This does not imply that every venue accepts the same file format or that native structure alone guarantees an accessible export. Inspect the destination’s current instructions and test the delivered format.
